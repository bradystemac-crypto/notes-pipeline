## Problem 1 (recording of the numerical component available in Canvas) – Let's estimate the rate of heat loss during breathing. An average person inhales about 6 L/min of bone-dry air at 20°C and exhales air saturated with water vapor at 37°C. For this exercise, let's assume steady state, no non-flow work, potential and kinetic energy changes are negligible, no reactions, air is ideal gas, and the mass of air is unchanged.

Information given: MW(air) 28.84 g/mol, $p_{air}$ = 0.0012 g/cm$^3$, saturated water vapor at 37°C contains 0.041 g water per 1.0g of dry air.

a) In your own words, describe the steps you will take in the enthalpy path.
b) Calculate the change in specific enthalpy that results from warming dry air from 20C to 37C (i.e. the first part of the hypothetical path).
c) Calculate the total change in enthalpy in this system (see figure for help on the hypothetical path).
d) Calculate the rate of heat transfer. Is this individual losing or gaining heat as they exercise?
e) The assumptions made for this problem are a simplification of exercising. Pick your favorite form of exercise, which assumptions would you change to create a more realistic scenario for your sport of choice? Explain your rationale behind each change.

**Hypothetical Path Diagram for Air and Water:**
- **Overall Path:** A solid arrow pointing from "Dry air 20 °C" to "Saturated air 37 °C" with "$\hat{\Delta H}$" above it.
- **Sensible Enthalpy Change:** A dashed arrow going down-right from "Dry air 20 °C" to "Dry air + water 37 °C" labeled "$\hat{\Delta H}_{sens}$".
- **Vaporization Enthalpy Change:** A dashed arrow going up-right from "Dry air + water 37 °C" to "Saturated air 37 °C" labeled "$\hat{\Delta H}_{vap}$".
- **Legend:** "---> Hypothetical path"

### Solution
Ⓐ You will need to heat up the dry air & then find the enthalpy from the vapor. of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$

Ⓑ
- $$\text{Air (g)} \xrightarrow{\boxed{\int \hat{C}_p dT}} \text{Air (g)}$$
  $$\text{@ } 20^\circ \text{C} \qquad\qquad \text{@ } 37^\circ \text{C}$$
- $$\hat{\Delta H}_{sens} = \int_{20}^{37} C_P(T) dT$$
- $$C_P(T) = 33.46 + 0.688 \times 10^{-2} t + 0.7604 \times 10^{-5} t^2 - 3.593 \times 10^{-9} t^3$$
- $$\hat{\Delta H}_{vap} \text{ @ } 37^\circ\text{C} = 2,414.2 \text{ J/g}$$
- Energy balance for open system:
  $$-\Delta\hat{H} + \dot{Q} + \dot{W}_{nf} = 0$$
- Given no non-flow work ($\dot{W}_{nf} = 0$):
  $$-\Delta\hat{H} + \dot{Q} = 0$$
- Total enthalpy change for hypothetical path:
  $$\boxed{\hat{\Delta H}_T = \hat{\Delta H}_1 + \hat{\Delta H}_2}$$

## Problem 2 – A researcher wants to raise the temperature of a steady stream of phosphate buffered saline (PBS) flowing at 20g/min. PBS is a water-based salt solution that is being used by the researcher to bathe a tissue sample. Her system has a mixer that inputs 50 J/min (non-flow W) and an electric heater with a coil immersed in the PBS. You can assume the heat capacity of PBS is identical to that of water, there are no reactions in the system, no potential or kinetic energy changes, and the system is at steady state.

* Write a mass accounting equation for this system.
* Write a simplified energy accounting equation for this system.
* Calculate the change in specific enthalpy in this system.
* Calculate the rate of heat required to continuously warm the stream of PBS from room temperature (25C) to 37C.

**Schematic System Diagram:**
- **System:** A dashed rectangular box containing "PBS".
- **Inlet:** A line on the left labeled "① in" with an arrow pointing into the box, annotated with "$20\text{ g/min}$".
- **Outlet:** An outlet line on the right labeled "② out" with an arrow pointing out of the box, annotated with "$20\text{ g/min}$".
- **Non-flow Work:** A squiggly arrow at the top labeled "$\dot{W}_{nf}$" entering the system.
- **Heat Transfer:** A squiggly arrow at the bottom labeled "$\dot{Q}?$" pointing towards the system.

---

### Solution
Ⓚ Mass accounting equation:
- $$\dot{M}_{in} - \dot{M}_{out} = 0$$
- $$\boxed{\dot{M}_{in} = \dot{M}_{out}}$$

Ⓑ Simplified energy accounting equation:
- $$-\dot{m}\hat{\Delta H} + \sum \dot{Q} + \sum \dot{W}_{nf} = 0$$
- $$\boxed{-\dot{m}\hat{\Delta H} + \dot{Q} + \dot{W}_{nf} = 0}$$

Ⓒ Rate of heat required:
- $$-20 \frac{\text{g}}{\text{min}} \left( 50.232 \frac{\text{J}}{\text{g}} \right) + \dot{Q} + 50 \text{ J/min} = 0$$
- $$\dot{Q} = -50 + 1004.64$$
- $$\dot{Q} = \boxed{954.64 \text{ J/min}}$$

Ⓓ Change in specific enthalpy:
- For liquid:
  $$\hat{\Delta H} = C_P \Delta T$$
- $$= 4.186 \frac{\text{J}}{\text{g} \cdot \text{[unclear: }^\circ\text{C or K]}} (37^\circ\text{C} - 25^\circ\text{C})$$
- $$= \boxed{50.232 \text{ J/g}}$$

# 📌 Key Concepts
- Conservation of Energy for Open Systems at Steady State.
- Mass Accounting Equation for Open Systems ($\dot{M}_{in} = \dot{M}_{out}$).
- Energy Accounting Equation for Open Systems: $-\dot{m}\hat{\Delta H} + \dot{Q} + \dot{W}_{nf} = 0$.
- Enthalpy Change for Sensible Heating: $\hat{\Delta H}_{sens} = \int C_P dT$.
- Enthalpy Change for Phase Transitions (Latent Heat of Vaporization): $\hat{\Delta H}_{vap}$.
- Specific Enthalpy and Heat Capacity ($C_P$) for ideal gases and liquids.
- Non-flow work ($\dot{W}_{nf}$) and Heat Transfer ($\dot{Q}$) in open systems.
- Assumptions in thermodynamic analysis (e.g., steady state, negligible KE/PE changes, ideal gas).
