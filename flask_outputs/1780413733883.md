CONSERVATION OF MASS IN STEADY-STATE NON-REACTING SYSTEMS WITH MULTIPLE INLETS AND OUTLETS

Problem 1
Two cylindrical venous capillary vessels join to form a larger cylindrical venule to return blood to the heart. The first vessel has a diameter of $0.0006 \, \text{cm}$, and an average blood flow velocity of $0.07 \, \text{cm/s}$. The second vessel has a diameter of $0.0007 \, \text{cm}$ and an average blood flow velocity of $0.08 \, \text{cm/s}$. The diameter of the collecting vessel is $0.0008 \, \text{cm}$.
Find the average blood flow velocity and the mass flow rate of blood in the collecting vessel. Assume blood density is unchanged.
$\boxed{\rho_{blood} = 1.056 \, \text{g/cm}^3}$

Draw:
[IMAGE: A diagram showing a flow junction. Two arrows labeled $\dot{M}_1$ and $\dot{M}_2$ enter a rectangular box representing the junction, and one arrow labeled $\dot{M}_3$ exits the box. The box is outlined by a dashed line labeled "System boundary". There are circled numbers labeling the inlet/outlet lines: ① for $\dot{M}_1$, ② for $\dot{M}_2$, and ③ for $\dot{M}_3$]

Assumptions:
- density = unchanged
- steady-state
- open system
- no rxns
- STP

Initial values:
$\dot{M}_1 \implies d_1 = 6 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{cm}$
$v_1 = 0.07 \, \text{cm/s}$
$\dot{M}_2 \implies d_2 = 7 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{cm}$
$v_2 = 0.08 \, \text{cm/s}$
$\dot{M}_3 \implies d_3 = 8 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{cm}$

Step ①:
$\boxed{\dot{M}_1 + \dot{M}_2 = \dot{M}_3}$
[This is the mass balance equation for steady-state, non-reacting, open systems, where the sum of mass flow rates in equals the sum of mass flow rates out.]
$A_1 v_1 + A_2 v_2 = A_3 v_3$
[Since density is unchanged, the volume flow rates ($A \cdot v$) can be balanced directly, as density will cancel out from both sides of the equation $\rho A_1 v_1 + \rho A_2 v_2 = \rho A_3 v_3$.]
$\pi\left(\frac{6 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{cm}}{2}\right)^2(0.07 \, \text{cm/s}) + \pi\left(\frac{7 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{cm}}{2}\right)^2(0.08 \, \text{cm/s}) = \pi\left(\frac{8 \times 10^{-4} \, \text{cm}}{2}\right)^2 v_3$
[Substitute the given diameters and velocities into the area ($A = \pi (D/2)^2$) and velocity terms to calculate the volume flow rates.]
$1.978 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{cm}^3\text{/s} + 3.077 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{cm}^3\text{/s} = 5.024 \times 10^{-7} \, \text{cm}^2 v_3$
[Calculate the individual volume flow rates for the inlet vessels and the area of the outlet vessel.]
$5.055 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{cm}^3\text{/s} = 5.024 \times 10^{-7} \, \text{cm}^2 (v_3)$
[Sum the inlet volume flow rates and set equal to the product of the outlet area and unknown velocity $v_3$.]
$\boxed{v_3 = 0.102 \, \text{cm/s}}$
[Solve for $v_3$, the average blood flow velocity in the collecting vessel.]

$\dot{M}_1 + \dot{M}_2 = \dot{M}_3$
[Reiterate the mass balance equation.]
$\rho v_1 A_1 + \rho v_2 A_2 = \rho v_3 A_3$
[Expand the mass flow rate terms using $\dot{M} = \rho A v$.]
$\rho v_1 A_1 + \rho v_2 A_2 = \dot{M}_3$
[Recognize that the sum of the mass flow rates of the inlets equals the mass flow rate of the outlet.]
$1.056 \, \text{g/cm}^3 (1.978 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{cm}^3\text{/s}) + 1.056 \, \text{g/cm}^3 (3.077 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{cm}^3\text{/s}) = 1.056 \, \text{g/cm}^3 (5.055 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{cm}^3\text{/s})$

$2.089 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{g/s} + 3.249 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{g/s} = 5.338 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{g/s}$
[Calculate the mass flow rates for each inlet and sum them.]
$\boxed{5.338 \, \text{g/s} = 5.338 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{g/s}}$
[The sum of inlet mass flow rates is the total mass flow rate $\dot{M}_3$ in the collecting vessel. Note: The final line of the box seems to have a typo where the boxed value has $5.338 \, \text{g/s}$ but the right side has $5.338 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{g/s}$. Based on the preceding line, $5.338 \times 10^{-8} \, \text{g/s}$ is the correct answer for $\dot{M}_3$.]

# 📌 Global Key Concepts
- **Conservation of Mass:** For steady-state, non-reacting, open systems, the total mass flow rate entering the system must equal the total mass flow rate leaving the system. This can be expressed as $\sum \dot{M}_{\text{in}} = \sum \dot{M}_{\text{out}}$.
- **Mass Flow Rate ($\dot{M}$):** The rate at which mass passes through a cross-sectional area, defined as the product of density ($\rho$), cross-sectional area ($A$), and average velocity ($v$): $\dot{M} = \rho A v$.
- **Volume Flow Rate ($\dot{V}$):** The rate at which volume passes through a cross-sectional area, defined as the product of cross-sectional area ($A$) and average velocity ($v$): $\dot{V} = A v$. In incompressible flow (constant density), conservation of mass simplifies to conservation of volume flow rate, $\sum \dot{V}_{\text{in}} = \sum \dot{V}_{\text{out}}$.
- **Steady-State System:** A system where all properties (like flow rates, concentrations, temperature) at any given point do not change with time.
- **Non-Reacting System:** A system where no chemical reactions occur, meaning mass is neither created nor destroyed within the system due to reactions.
- **Bypass Grafting:** A surgical procedure to reroute blood flow around an obstructed artery, re-establishing adequate blood supply to the affected region.

# 📐 Global Key Equations
- **Mass Balance for Steady-State, Non-Reacting System:**
    $$ \sum \dot{M}_{\text{in}} = \sum \dot{M}_{\text{out}} $$
- **Mass Flow Rate:**
    $$ \dot{M} = \rho A v $$
    where $\rho$ is density, $A$ is cross-sectional area, and $v$ is average velocity.
- **Area of a Circular Vessel:**
    $$ A = \pi \left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^2 $$
    where $D$ is the diameter.
