# 📌 Key Concepts
-   **Module 9 Outline:** Focuses on conservation of energy in open non-reacting systems. Key objectives include describing enthalpy and its relation to internal energy, differentiating state and path functions, understanding heat capacity, calculating enthalpy changes due to mixing, temperature, and phase changes, and applying the conservation of total energy equation.
-   **Enthalpy (H):** A thermodynamic function defined as $H = U + PV$. It combines internal energy ($U$) and flow work ($PV$) into a single term.
    -   **Rates:** $\dot{H} = \dot{U} + P\dot{V}$
    -   **Specific Enthalpy:** $\hat{H} = \hat{u} + P\hat{v}$
-   Like internal energy, enthalpy cannot be known in absolute terms; only changes ($\Delta \hat{H}$) can be calculated.
-   **State Function:** A property that depends only on the current state of the system and not on the path taken to reach that state. Examples include temperature, specific enthalpy ($\hat{H}$), and pressure. For instance, changing temperature from $15^\circ\text{C}$ to $60^\circ\text{C}$ directly or via $85^\circ\text{C}$ results in the same $\Delta T$.
-   **Path Function:** A property that depends on the path taken between states. Examples include heat and work.
-   **Internal Energy (U):** Represents the molecular motion and potential chemical energy from forces within and between molecules in a system.
-   **Internal Energy (U) vs. Enthalpy (H):** Both $U$ and $H$ are state functions. They cannot be calculated as absolute values, only differences between two states. Through energy accounting, the amount of work and heat can be calculated.
-   **Calculating Enthalpy Changes:** Since $\Delta\hat{H}$ is a state function, the change between two states can be calculated using any convenient hypothetical path, as the overall change is independent of the path. The total change in $\hat{H}$ across a system is the sum of all steps in a hypothetical path: $\Delta\hat{H} = \sum_k \Delta\hat{H}_k$.
-   **Common Factors that Change Enthalpy:**
    -   **Temperature:** Changes in temperature involve "sensible heat," which is the energy required to raise or lower the temperature of a material, equivalent to the $\Delta H$ associated with that temperature change.
    -   **Phase Changes:** (Implicitly covered by the provided table of heats of phase change).
-   **Heat Capacity ($C_p$):** Describes the relationship between changes in enthalpy and temperature. It is defined as the slope of the $\Delta\hat{H}$ vs. $T$ curve under constant pressure conditions.
    -   For **solids and liquids**, $C_p(T)$ is often dominated by its first constant term 'a', simplifying $\Delta\hat{H} \approx C_p \Delta T$.
    -   For **gases**, it's typically necessary to integrate a polynomial expression for $C_p(T)$ over the temperature range. Coefficients for $C_p(T)$ are looked up in tables.

# 📐 Key Equations
-   Enthalpy definition:
    $$H = U + PV$$
-   Rates of enthalpy change:
    $$\dot{H} = \dot{U} + P\dot{V}$$
-   Specific enthalpy:
    $$\hat{H} = \hat{u} + P\hat{v}$$
-   Change in specific enthalpy between states A and B:
    $$\Delta\hat{H} = \hat{H}_B - \hat{H}_A$$
-   Total change in specific enthalpy as a sum of hypothetical steps:
    $$\Delta\hat{H} = \sum_k \Delta\hat{H}_k$$
-   Example of total specific enthalpy change over two steps:
    $$\Delta\hat{H}_{\text{tot}} = \Delta\hat{H}_1 + \Delta\hat{H}_2$$
-   Definition of heat capacity ($C_p$) as a limit:
    $$C_p(T) = \lim_{\Delta T \rightarrow 0} \frac{\Delta\hat{H}}{\Delta T} = \text{slope of curve}$$
-   Enthalpy change via integration of heat capacity over temperature:
    $$\Delta\hat{H} = \int_{T_0}^{T_f} C_p(T) dT$$
-   Polynomial representation of heat capacity:
    $$C_p(T) = a + bT + cT^2 + dT^3 \dots$$
-   Approximate enthalpy change for solids and liquids:
    $$\Delta\hat{H} \approx C_p \Delta T$$
