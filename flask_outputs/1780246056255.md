## Problem 2 – A researcher wants to raise the temperature of a steady stream of phosphate buffered saline (PBS) flowing at 20g/min. PBS is a water-based salt solution that is being used by the researcher to bathe a tissue sample. Her system has a mixer that inputs 50 J/min (non-flow W) and an electric heater with a coil immersed in the PBS. You can assume the heat capacity of PBS is identical to that of water, there are no reactions in the system, no potential or kinetic energy changes, and the system is at steady state.

*   **Write a mass accounting equation for this system.**
*   **Write a simplified energy accounting equation for this system.**
*   **Calculate the change in specific enthalpy in this system.**
*   **Calculate the rate of heat required to continuously warm the stream of PBS from room temperature (25C) to 37C.**

**[System Boundary Diagram]:** A dashed rectangular box labeled "PBS" (with a tilde). An input arrow enters from the left, labeled ①, "in", and "20 g/min". An output arrow exits to the right, labeled ②, "out", and "20 g/min". A wavy arrow enters from the top, labeled "$\dot{W}_{nf}$". A wavy arrow enters from the bottom, labeled "$\dot{Q}?$".

### Handwritten Notes for Problem 2

- Ⓚ:
  - $$\dot{m}_{in} - \dot{m}_{out} = 0$$
  - $$\boxed{\dot{m}_{in} = \dot{m}_{out}}$$
- Ⓑ:
  - $$-\dot{m} \Delta \hat{H} + \sum \dot{Q} + \sum \dot{W}_{nf} = 0$$
  - $$\boxed{-\dot{m}\Delta\hat{H} + \dot{Q} + \dot{W}_{nf} = 0}$$
- Ⓒ:
  - $$-20 \frac{\text{g}}{\text{min}} \left(50.232 \frac{\text{J}}{\text{g}}\right) + \dot{Q} + 50 \frac{\text{J}}{\text{min}} = 0$$
  - $$\dot{Q} = -50 + 1004.64$$
  - $$\boxed{\dot{Q} = 954.64 \text{ J/min}}$$
- Ⓓ:
  - Liquid:
  - $$\Delta\hat{H} = C_p \Delta T$$
  - $$= 4.186 \frac{\text{J}}{\text{g}\cdot\text{°K}} (37\text{°C} - 25\text{°C})$$
  - $$\boxed{= 50.232 \text{ J/g}}$$

# 📌 Key Concepts
- Conservation of Energy in Open Systems (First Law of Thermodynamics).
- Steady state assumption for mass and energy balances.
- Enthalpy changes in a system, including sensible heat (temperature change) and latent heat (phase change, e.g., vaporization).
- Hypothetical path approach for calculating total enthalpy change.
- Specific heat capacity ($C_p$) for calculating sensible enthalpy change.
- Non-flow work ($\dot{W}_{nf}$) and heat transfer ($\dot{Q}$) in an open system.
- Mass balance for open systems at steady state.

# 📐 Key Equations
- General energy balance for open systems:
  $$-\dot{m}\Delta\hat{H} + \sum \dot{Q} + \sum \dot{W}_{nf} = 0$$
- Simplified energy balance (assuming steady state, negligible potential/kinetic energy, no reactions):
  $$-\dot{m}\Delta\hat{H} + \dot{Q} + \dot{W}_{nf} = 0$$
- Total enthalpy change via hypothetical path:
  $$\Delta\hat{H}_T = \Delta\hat{H}_{sens} + \Delta\hat{H}_{vap}$$
- Sensible enthalpy change for a gas (where $C_p$ is a function of temperature):
  $$\Delta \hat{H}_{sens} = \int_{T_1}^{T_2} C_p(T) dT$$
- Specific heat capacity polynomial (for dry air):
  $$C_p(T) = 33.46 + 0.688 \times 10^{-2} T + 0.7604 \times 10^{-5} T^2 - 3.593 \times 10^{-9} T^3$$
- Enthalpy of vaporization of water at 37°C:
  $$\Delta \hat{H}_{vap} = 2,414.2 \text{ J/g}$$
- Specific enthalpy change for a liquid:
  $$\Delta\hat{H} = C_p \Delta T$$
- Specific heat capacity of water:
  $$C_p = 4.186 \frac{\text{J}}{\text{g}\cdot\text{°K}}$$
- Mass balance for steady state open systems:
  $$\dot{m}_{in} = \dot{m}_{out}$$
