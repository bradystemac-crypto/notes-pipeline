**Problem 1**
A bioreactor loaded with mammalian cells operates continuously to produce glutamic acid ($C_5H_9NO_4$) from glucose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$) and ammonia ($NH_3$) in the following balanced reaction:

$$1.0 C_6H_{12}O_6 + 0.2 NH_3 + 2.85 O_2 \rightarrow 0.7 C_5H_9NO_4 + 2.5 CO_2 + 3.8 H_2O \quad \text{Balanced}$$

Given information:
* The system is steady state, continuous, with a reaction
* Glucose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$) is delivered at 100.0 mol/day.
* The outlet molar flow rate of ammonia ($NH_3$) is 580 mol/day.
* The ratio of the outlet molar flow rate of glucose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$) to the outlet molar flow rate of ammonia ($NH_3$) is 0.0935. In other words: $\frac{\dot{n}_{out, glucose}}{\dot{n}_{out, NH_3}} = 0.0935$
  [^1]
  [^1]: This ratio directly provides a relationship between the outlet flow rates of glucose and ammonia.

(a) Write molar balance equations for all three reactants.
(b) Calculate the reaction rate in moles/day.
(c) Calculate the outlet molar flow rate of glutamic acid ($C_5H_9NO_4$) in units of mols/day.

# 📌 Global Key Concepts
- **Steady-State Systems:** Input rates equal output rates plus/minus generation/consumption rates.
- **Molar Balance:** The fundamental principle for tracking material flow in a reactor, equating input, output, generation, and consumption for each species.
- **Stoichiometry:** The quantitative relationship between reactants and products in a balanced chemical reaction, defined by stoichiometric coefficients ($\Theta_s$).
- **Reaction Rate (R):** The rate at which a reaction proceeds, typically expressed in moles per unit time, directly proportional to the change in moles of any species divided by its stoichiometric coefficient.
- **Fractional Conversion ($f_s$):** A measure of the extent of a reaction, defined as the fraction of the inlet moles of a reactant that has been consumed.
- **Limiting Reactant (LR):** The reactant that is completely consumed first, thereby limiting the maximum extent of a reaction. Determined by the smallest ratio of initial moles to its absolute stoichiometric coefficient.
- **Respiratory Quotient (RQ):** The ratio of moles of $CO_2$ produced to moles of $O_2$ consumed, particularly relevant in biological systems.
- **Mass Conservation:** The principle that total mass within a closed system or control volume remains constant over time. For continuous steady-state systems, total mass in equals total mass out.

# 📐 Global Key Equations
* **General Molar Balance for species $s$ in a steady-state system:**
  $$\dot{n}_{in, s} - \dot{n}_{out, s} + \Theta_s R = 0$$
  where $\dot{n}$ is molar flow rate, $R$ is the reaction rate, and $\Theta_s$ is the stoichiometric coefficient (negative for reactants, positive for products).

* **Reaction Rate ($R$) from Molar Balance:**
  $$R = \frac{\dot{n}_{in, s} - \dot{n}_{out, s}}{-\Theta_s}$$

* **Fractional Conversion ($f_s$):**
  $$f_s = \frac{\dot{n}_{in, s} - \dot{n}_{out, s}}{\dot{n}_{in, s}}$$

* **Reaction Rate ($R$) from Fractional Conversion:**
  $$R = \frac{\dot{n}_{in, s} \cdot f_s}{-\Theta_s}$$

* **Limiting Reactant (LR) Determination:**
  $$LR = \min \left| \frac{\dot{n}_{in, s}}{-\Theta_s} \right|$$

* **Respiratory Quotient (RQ):**
  $$RQ = \frac{\dot{n}_{out, CO_2}}{\dot{n}_{in, O_2}}$$

* **Molecular Weight ($Mw$) Calculation:**
  $$Mw = \sum (\text{atomic weight of element } i \times \text{number of atoms of element } i)$$
