# 📌 Key Concepts
-   **Module 9 Outline:** Focuses on the conservation of energy in open, non-reacting systems. Key topics include enthalpy, internal energy, state vs. path functions, heat capacity, and calculating enthalpy changes due to mixing, temperature, pressure, and phase changes.
-   **Enthalpy (H):** A thermodynamic function defined as $H = U + PV$.
    -   It combines internal energy ($U$) and flow work ($PV$) into a single term.
    -   Like internal energy, enthalpy cannot be known in absolute terms; only changes ($\Delta H$) can be calculated.
    -   Rates and specific enthalpy are represented as $\dot{H} = \dot{U} + P\dot{V}$ and $\hat{H} = \hat{U} + P\hat{V}$, respectively.
-   **State vs. Path Functions:**
    -   **State Function:** A property that depends only on the current state of the system, not on the path taken to reach that state. Examples include temperature, specific enthalpy ($\hat{H}$), and pressure. For instance, a temperature change from $15^\circ\text{C}$ to $85^\circ\text{C}$ then to $60^\circ\text{C}$ results in the same net $\Delta T$ as going directly from $15^\circ\text{C}$ to $60^\circ\text{C}$.
    -   **Path Function:** A property whose value depends on the sequence of steps taken to change the system from one state to another. Examples include heat and work.
-   **Internal Energy (U) vs. Enthalpy (H):** Both are state functions and cannot be calculated in absolute terms, but the differences between two states can be calculated. Through energy accounting, the amount of work and heat can be determined.
-   **Calculating Enthalpy Changes:**
    -   Since specific enthalpy ($\Delta \hat{H}$) is a state function, the path chosen to convert a system from one state to another does not affect the overall change.
    -   The total change in specific enthalpy across a system is the sum of the enthalpy changes for all steps in a hypothetical path: $\Delta \hat{H} = \sum_k \Delta \hat{H}_k$.
-   **Common Factors Changing Enthalpy:**
    -   **Temperature (Sensible Heat):** The energy required to raise or lower the temperature of a material, equivalent to the change in enthalpy associated with that temperature change.
    -   **Heat Capacity ($C_p$):** Describes the relationship between enthalpy change and temperature. It is defined as $C_p(T) = \lim_{\Delta T \to 0} \frac{\Delta \hat{H}}{\Delta T}$, representing the slope of the $\Delta \hat{H}$ vs. T curve at constant pressure.
        -   The change in specific enthalpy due to temperature change is calculated by integrating heat capacity over the temperature range: $\Delta \hat{H} = \int_{T_0}^{T_f} C_p(T) dT$.
        -   $C_p(T)$ is often expressed as a polynomial: $a + bT + cT^2 + dT^3\dots$, with coefficients looked up in tables.
        -   For solids and liquids, $C_p(T)$ is often approximated by its first term 'a', leading to $\Delta \hat{H} = C_p(T_f - T_0) \approx a(T_f - T_0) \approx a\Delta T$.
        -   For gases, the full polynomial integration of $C_p(T)$ is typically required.
    -   **Phase Changes:** Enthalpy changes occur during phase transitions (e.g., melting, vaporization), as indicated by the provided table of standard heats of phase change ($\Delta\hat{H}_m$ for melting, $\Delta\hat{H}_v$ for vaporization). These are usually given at specific temperatures and 1 atm pressure.

# 📐 Key Equations
-   **Enthalpy Definition:**
    $$H = U + PV$$
-   **Rates of Enthalpy:**
    $$\dot{H} = \dot{U} + P\dot{V}$$
-   **Specific Enthalpy:**
    $$\hat{H} = \hat{U} + P\hat{V}$$
-   **Change in Specific Enthalpy:**
    $$\Delta \hat{H} = \hat{H}_B - \hat{H}_A$$
-   **Total Change in Enthalpy (sum of steps):**
    $$\Delta \hat{H} = \sum_k \Delta \hat{H}_k$$
-   **Total Change in Enthalpy (two steps example):**
    $$\Delta \hat{H}_{\text{tot}} = \Delta \hat{H}_1 + \Delta \hat{H}_2$$
-   **Heat Capacity Definition:**
    $$C_p(T) = \lim_{\Delta T \to 0} \frac{\Delta \hat{H}}{\Delta T}$$
-   **Enthalpy Change due to Temperature (general):**
    $$\Delta \hat{H} = \int_{T_0}^{T_f} C_p(T) dT$$
-   **Heat Capacity Polynomial:**
    $$C_p(T) = a + bT + cT^2 + dT^3\dots$$
-   **Enthalpy Change due to Temperature (solids/liquids approximation):**
    $$\Delta \hat{H} = C_p(T_f - T_0) \approx a(T_f - T_0) \approx a\Delta T$$

# 📋 Summary
This module introduces the concept of enthalpy ($H = U + PV$) as a key thermodynamic function for open, non-reacting systems, combining internal energy and flow work. It distinguishes between state functions (like enthalpy and temperature, which depend only on the current state) and path functions (like heat and work, which depend on the process path). The notes explain that while absolute enthalpy cannot be measured, changes in enthalpy ($\Delta \hat{H}$) can be calculated by summing changes along any convenient hypothetical path. Key factors influencing enthalpy changes, such as temperature (sensible heat) and heat capacity ($C_p$), are discussed, along with equations for calculating $\Delta \hat{H}$ for solids, liquids, and gases, and recognition of enthalpy changes during phase transitions.
