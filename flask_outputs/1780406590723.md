# 📝 Problems & Solutions
**Problem Set 8.2 – Conservation of Energy in Closed Systems**

**Problem 1** – An autoclave in a hospital is used for sterilizing surgical instruments at an operational temperature ($T_{sys}$) of $121^\circ\text{C}$. While the autoclave is designed to be a closed system, it is not perfectly insulated, allowing for some heat exchange with the surrounding air at a temperature ($T_{surr}$) of $20^\circ\text{C}$. Even though the pressure inside the autoclave increases as it is heated, its volume remains constant ($\rightarrow W_{nF} = 0$). Assume the autoclave has a surface area through which heat is lost of $2\text{ m}^2$ and a heat transfer coefficient ($h$) of $10\text{ W/m}^2\cdot^\circ\text{C}$.

*   What is the **change in internal energy** of the autoclave?
*   Is there **heat loss or gain** by the autoclave in this environment?

Step ①:
- [IMAGE: A diagram showing a dashed rectangular border representing a closed system. Inside the box, the hand-written text "$121^\circ\text{C}$" and "SYSTEM" are present. Below the box, "$20^\circ\text{C}$" is written. A wavy red arrow points outwards from the top of the box, labeled with "$\dot{Q}$"]

Step ②: Assumptions:
- $W_{nF} = 0 \ (\Delta V = 0)$
- NO $\Delta EP / \Delta EK$
- Closed system

Step ③:
- $$\dot{\Delta u}_{sys} = \sum \dot{Q} + \cancel{\sum \dot{w}_{nF}}^0 \quad \text{General Form}$$
  - $\dot{Q} = \dot{\Delta u}_{sys}$ (Dynamic)
  - _Side note:_ steady state when temp. of system doesn't change

Step ④:
- $$\dot{q} = h A (T_{surr} - T_{sys})$$
- $$= 10 \frac{\text{W}}{\text{m}^2\cdot^\circ\text{C}} \cdot 2\text{ m}^2 (20^\circ\text{C} - 121^\circ\text{C})$$
  - $$= -2,020\text{ W}$$

Step ⑤:
- $$\dot{\Delta u} = \dot{q} + \dot{w}_{nF}$$
- $$= -2,020\text{ W} + 0$$
- $$= -2,020\text{ W}$$
  - _Conclusion:_ Heat & energy are lost in the environment

---

**Problem 2** – In a biomedical engineering research facility, a reaction occurs in a sealed device with a movable piston, synthesizing a gas used for medical purposes. The system absorbs 1000 J of heat. Initially, the container holds of gas at a temperature of $320\text{ K}$, an initial volume of $2\text{ L}$, and a pressure of $2\text{ atm}$. The pressure remains constant due to the piston's movement. The change in internal energy of the gas ($\Delta U$) during the reaction is $300\text{ J}$.

*   Calculate the **final volume** of the gas after the reaction.
*   The device is designed so that containers of multiple sizes (2L, 5L or 10L) can be used depending on the application. The container currently being used holds up to 5L. **Is it safe** to use this container? Would you recommend using a different one? *Justify your answer*

Step ①:
- [IMAGE: A diagram showing a dashed rectangular box labeled "Device". On the left side, a red wavy arrow points inward with the label "$Q$". On the right side, a red wavy arrow points outward with the label "$\text{↳? } W_{nF}$".]

Step ②: Assumptions:
- Closed system
- NO $\Delta EP / \Delta EK$

Step ③:
- $$\Delta U_{sys} = \sum Q + \sum W_{nF}$$
- $$300\text{ J} = 1000\text{ J} - P (V_f - V_i)$$
- $$300\text{ J} = 1000\text{ J} - \left[ (2\text{ atm})(V_f - 2\text{ L}) \right]$$
- $$700\text{ J} = 2\text{ atm}(V_f - 2\text{ L})$$
  - _Conversion:_ $101.325\text{ J} = 1\text{ atm}\cdot\text{L}$
- $$700\text{ J} \times \frac{1\text{ atm}\cdot\text{L}}{101.325\text{ J}} = 6.91\text{ atm}\cdot\text{L}$$
- $$6.91\text{ atm}\cdot\text{L} = 2\text{ atm}(V_f - 2\text{ L})$$
- $$3.45\text{ L} = V_f - 2\text{ L}$$
  - $$V_f = 5.45\text{ L}$$

Step ④:
- _Safety Conclusion:_ NO, the final volume is $5.45\text{ L}$, so we should use the $10\text{ L}$ device.

# 📋 Summary
- **Problem 1:** Calculated the change in internal energy of an autoclave operating at $121^\circ\text{C}$ with $2\text{ m}^2$ surface area and $h=10\text{ W/m}^2\cdot^\circ\text{C}$ in a $20^\circ\text{C}$ environment. The change in internal energy was $-2,020\text{ W}$, indicating heat and energy are lost to the environment.
- **Problem 2:** Determined the final volume of a gas reacting in a sealed device with a movable piston, absorbing $1000\text{ J}$ of heat and having a $\Delta U$ of $300\text{ J}$. Given an initial volume of $2\text{ L}$ at $2\text{ atm}$ constant pressure, the final volume was calculated to be $5.45\text{ L}$. This means the $5\text{ L}$ container currently in use is unsafe, and a $10\text{ L}$ container should be used instead.

# 📌 Key Concepts
- Conservation of Energy for closed systems.
- Internal energy change ($\Delta U$ or $\dot{\Delta u}$).
- Heat transfer rate ($\dot{Q}$ or $\dot{q}$) via convection.
- Non-flow work ($W_{nF}$ or $\dot{w}_{nF}$) in systems with constant volume or constant pressure.
- Pressure-volume work calculation.
- Conversion between energy units ($J$) and pressure-volume units ($\text{atm}\cdot\text{L}$).

# 📐 Key Equations
- General Energy Balance for a Closed System (rate form):
  $$\dot{\Delta u}_{sys} = \sum \dot{Q} + \sum \dot{w}_{nF}$$
- General Energy Balance for a Closed System (total change):
  $$\Delta U_{sys} = \sum Q + \sum W_{nF}$$
- Convective Heat Transfer Rate:
  $$\dot{q} = h A (T_{surr} - T_{sys})$$
- Non-Flow Work (constant pressure):
  $$W_{nF} = -P \Delta V = -P(V_f - V_i)$$
- Conversion Factor:
  $$1\text{ atm}\cdot\text{L} = 101.325\text{ J}$$
