# 📝 Problems & Solutions

**Problem 1 – Liquid nitrogen is used in a number of medical applications, such as removing tumors during surgery. Suppose gaseous N2 at room temperature (298 K) is cooled to liquid > gas N2 just below the boiling point (77 K). The specific enthalpy change to cool nitrogen from 298 K to 77 K is -1435 cal/mol. The heat of vaporization, the specific enthalpy change from the liquid to the vapor form, of nitrogen is 1336 cal/mol.**

What would be the overall change in specific enthalpy for this process?

- [IMAGE: Thermodynamic pathway diagram showing the transition of nitrogen from gas to liquid state]
  - Horizontal pathway: $N_2 \text{ (g)} \xrightarrow{\Delta\hat{H}} N_2 \text{ (l)}$ from $298\text{ K}$ to $77\text{ K}$
  - Vertical cooling pathway on the left: $N_2 \text{ (g)} \text{ @ } 298\text{ K}$ [downward red arrow labeled $\Delta\hat{H}_1$ with a "Cooling" highlight box] to $N_2 \text{ (g)} \text{ @ } 77\text{ K}$
  - Diagonal phase change pathway from bottom-left to top-right: $N_2 \text{ (g)} \text{ @ } 77\text{ K} \xrightarrow{\Delta\hat{H}_2} N_2 \text{ (l)} \text{ @ } 77\text{ K}$
- Handwritten Calculations:
  - $\Delta\hat{H}_1 = 298\text{ K to } 77\text{ K} = -1435\text{ cal/mol}$
  - $\Delta\hat{H}_2 = \text{Phase change} = -1336\text{ cal/mol}$
  - $\Delta\hat{H} = \Delta\hat{H}_1 + \Delta\hat{H}_2$
  - $= \boxed{-2771\text{ cal/mol}}$
  - $\underline{\underline{\text{exothermic}}}$

**b) Calculate the enthalpy change on a per-gram basis for the vaporization of sweat at 37°C.**

- Handwritten Calculations:
  - Step ⓐ:
    - [IMAGE: Thermodynamic pathway diagram for the vaporization of water from liquid at 37°C to vapor at 37°C]
      - Horizontal main path: $H_2O \text{ (l)} \xrightarrow{\Delta\hat{H}} H_2O \text{ (g)}$ from $37\text{°C}$ to $37\text{°C}$
      - Downward heating path on the left: $H_2O \text{ (l)} \text{ @ } 37\text{°C}$ [downward red vertical arrow labeled $\Delta\hat{H}_1$ and "① heat up liquid"] to $H_2O \text{ (l)} \text{ @ } 100\text{°C}$
      - Horizontal vaporization path at 100°C: $H_2O \text{ (l)} \text{ @ } 100\text{°C} \xrightarrow{\Delta\hat{H}_{vap} \text{ and "② vaporization"}} H_2O \text{ (g)} \text{ @ } 100\text{°C}$
      - Upward cooling path on the right: [upward red vertical arrow labeled $\Delta\hat{H}_3$ and "③ cool down gas"] to $H_2O \text{ (g)} \text{ @ } 37\text{°C}$
    - $$\Delta\hat{H} = \Delta\hat{H}_1 + \Delta\hat{H}_{vap} + \Delta\hat{H}_3$$
    - $$= 263.9\text{ J/g} + 2257\text{ J/g} - 118.09\text{ J/g}$$
    - $$= \boxed{2,401.616\text{ J/g}}$$
  - Calculations for $\Delta\hat{H}_3$:
    - $$\Delta\hat{H}_3 = \int_{100}^{37} \left( 33.46 + 0.00688 t + 0.7604 \times 10^{-5} t^2 - 3.593 \times 10^{-9} t^3 \right) dt$$
    - $$= \left[ 33.46 t + \frac{0.00688 t^2}{2} \right]_{100}^{37} + \left[ \frac{0.7604 \times 10^{-5} t^3}{3} \right]_{100}^{37} - \left[ \frac{3.593 \times 10^{-9} t^4}{4} \right]_{100}^{37}$$
    - $$= - 33.46(100) + 33.46(37) - \frac{0.00688(100)^2}{2} + \frac{0.00688(37)^2}{2}$$
    - $$- \frac{0.7604 \times 10^{-5}(100)^3}{3} + \frac{0.7604 \times 10^{-5}(37)^3}{3}$$
    - $$+ \frac{3.593 \times 10^{-9}(100)^4}{4} - \frac{3.593 \times 10^{-9}(37)^4}{4}$$
    - $$\Delta\hat{H}_3 = -2140\text{ J/mol} \times \frac{1\text{ mol}}{18\text{ g}}$$
    - $$= \boxed{-118.09\text{ J/g}}$$
  - Calculations for $\Delta\hat{H}_1$:
    - $$\Delta\hat{H}_1 = \int C_p dT$$
    - $$= 75.4\text{ J/(mol}\cdot\text{°C)} \times (100 - 37)\text{°C}$$
    - $$= 4750.2\text{ J/mol} \times \boxed{\frac{1\text{ mol}}{18\text{ g}}}$$
    - $$\Delta\hat{H}_1 = \boxed{263.9\text{ J/g}}$$

# 📌 Key Concepts
- **Specific Enthalpy Change ($\Delta\hat{H}$):** The energy change per unit mass or mole of a substance undergoing a process.
- **Heat of Vaporization ($\Delta\hat{H}_{vap}$):** The specific enthalpy change required for a substance to transition from its liquid phase to its gaseous phase at a constant temperature and pressure.
- **Thermodynamic Pathway:** A conceptual sequence of intermediate steps used to calculate the overall enthalpy change of a process, based on Hess's Law.
- **Heat Capacity ($C_p$):** The amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of a given substance by a specific amount.
- **Integration of Heat Capacity:** Used to calculate enthalpy change when heat capacity varies with temperature: $\Delta\hat{H} = \int C_p(T) dT$.
- **Steady State:** A condition in a system where all properties remain constant over time, implying no accumulation or depletion of mass or energy within the system.
- **Mass and Energy Accounting:** Fundamental balance equations used to analyze processes in terms of conservation of mass and energy.

# 📐 Key Equations
- Overall enthalpy change for a multi-step process (Hess's Law):
  $$ \Delta\hat{H}_{\text{total}} = \sum_{i} \Delta\hat{H}_i $$
- Enthalpy change for a temperature change (variable heat capacity):
  $$ \Delta\hat{H} = \int_{T_1}^{T_2} C_p(T) dT $$
- Polynomial form of heat capacity (as given in TABLE 4.1 for water vapor, where $t$ is in °C):
  $$ C_p(t) = a + bt + ct^2 + dt^3 $$
- Enthalpy change for a temperature change (constant heat capacity, as given for liquid water in TABLE 4.1):
  $$ \Delta\hat{H} = C_p \times (T_2 - T_1) $$
