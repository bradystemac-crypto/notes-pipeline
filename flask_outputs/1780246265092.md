**Problem 1 (recording of the numerical component available in Canvas) – Let's estimate the rate of heat loss during breathing. An average person inhales about 6 L/min of bone-dry air at 20°C and exhales air saturated with water vapor at 37°C. For this exercise, let's assume steady state, no non-flow work, potential and kinetic energy changes are negligible, no reactions, air is ideal gas, and the mass of air is unchanged.**

**Information given: MW(air) 28.84 g/mol, $\rho_{air}$ = 0.0012 g/cm³, saturated water vapor at 37°C contains 0.041 g water per 1.0g of dry air.**

A hypothetical path shows dry air at 20 °C undergoing a first step to dry air with water at 37 °C (labeled $\Delta \hat{H}_{sens}$), followed by a second step to saturated air at 37 °C (labeled $\Delta \hat{H}_{vap}$).]

*   **a) In your own words, describe the steps you will take in the enthalpy path.**
    *   Ⓐ You will need to heat up the dry air & then find the enthalpy from the vapor. of $H_2O$
*   **b) Calculate the change in specific enthalpy that results from warming dry air from 20C to 37C (i.e. the first part of the hypothetical path).**
    *   Ⓑ [IMAGE: Handwritten simple block diagram showing a process step: Air (g) @ 20°C to Air (g) @ 37°C with an integral of C_p over the arrow.]
    *   $$\Delta \hat{H}_{sens} = \int_{20}^{37} C_p(T) dt$$
    *   $$= 33.46 + 0.688 \times 10^{-2} t + 0.7604 \times 10^{-5} t^2 - 3.593 \times 10^{-9} t^3$$
*   **c) Calculate the total change in enthalpy in this system (see figure for help on the hypothetical path)**
    *   $$\Delta \hat{H}_{vap} @ 37°C = 2,414.2 \text{ J/g}$$
    *   $$\boxed{\Delta\hat{H}_T = \Delta\hat{H}_1 + \Delta\hat{H}_2}$$
*   **d) Calculate the rate of heat transfer. Is this individual losing or gaining heat as they exercise?**
    *   $$-\dot{m}\Delta\hat{H} + \dot{Q} + \dot{W}_{nf} = 0$$
    *   Given assumptions (no non-flow work, steady state, negligible potential/kinetic energy changes):
    *   $$-\Delta\hat{H} + \dot{Q} = 0$$

# Problem 2 – A researcher wants to raise the temperature of a steady stream of phosphate buffered saline (PBS) flowing at 20g/min. PBS is a water-based salt solution that is being used by the researcher to bathe a tissue sample. Her system has a mixer that inputs 50 J/min (non-flow W) and an electric heater with a coil immersed in the PBS. You can assume the heat capacity of PBS is identical to that of water, there are no reactions in the system, no potential or kinetic energy changes, and the system is at steady state.**

[IMAGE: Handwritten system boundary diagram. A dashed rectangular box labeled "PBS" with input "in" (20 g/min, ①) on the left and output "out" (20 g/min, ②) on the right. Wavy arrows indicate energy transfers: $\dot{W}_{nf}$ entering from the top and $\dot{Q}?$ entering from the bottom.]

*   **Write a mass accounting equation for this system.**
    *   Ⓚ $$\dot{m}_{in} - \dot{m}_{out} = 0$$
    *   $$\boxed{\dot{m}_{in} = \dot{m}_{out}}$$
*   **Write a simplified energy accounting equation for this system.**
    *   Ⓑ $$-\dot{m} \Delta \hat{H} + \sum \dot{Q} + \sum \dot{W}_{nf} = 0$$
    *   $$\boxed{-\dot{m}\Delta\hat{H} + \dot{Q} + \dot{W}_{nf} = 0}$$
*   **Calculate the change in specific enthalpy in this system.**
    *   Ⓓ Liquid:
    *   $$\Delta\hat{H} = C_p \Delta T$$
    *   $$= 4.186 \frac{\text{J}}{\text{g}\cdot\text{°K}} (37\text{°C} - 25\text{°C})$$
    *   $$\boxed{= 50.232 \text{ J/g}}$$
*   **Calculate the rate of heat required to continuously warm the stream of PBS from room temperature (25C) to 37C.**
    *   Ⓒ $$-20 \frac{\text{g}}{\text{min}} \left(50.232 \frac{\text{J}}{\text{g}}\right) + \dot{Q} + 50 \frac{\text{J}}{\text{min}} = 0$$
    *   $$\dot{Q} = -50 + 1004.64$$
    *   $$\boxed{\dot{Q} = 954.64 \text{ J/min}}$$

# 📌 Key Concepts
- **Conservation of Energy in Open Systems:** Application of the general energy balance equation for systems with mass flow.
- **Enthalpy Change ($\Delta \hat{H}$):** Calculation of specific enthalpy changes due to temperature variations (sensible heat) and phase changes (latent heat of vaporization).
- **Steady State:** Conditions where system properties do not change with time, simplifying energy balance equations.
- **Heat Capacity ($C_p$):** Used to calculate sensible heat changes in liquids and gases.
- **Non-Flow Work ($\dot{W}_{nf}$):** Work done on or by the system that is not associated with fluid flow (e.g., a mixer).
- **Hypothetical Path:** Decomposing a complex process into simpler, calculable steps for enthalpy determination.
- **Mass Accounting:** Application of mass conservation for open systems at steady state, where mass in equals mass out.

# 📐 Key Equations
- **General Energy Balance for Open Systems:**
    $$-\dot{m}\Delta\hat{H} + \dot{Q} + \dot{W}_{nf} = 0$$
- **Specific Enthalpy Change (Sensible Heat):**
    $$\Delta\hat{H}_{sens} = \int_{T_1}^{T_2} C_p(T) dT$$
- **Specific Enthalpy Change (for Liquids/Solids with constant $C_p$):**
    $$\Delta\hat{H} = C_p \Delta T$$
- **Total Enthalpy Change (Hypothetical Path):**
    $$\Delta\hat{H}_T = \Delta\hat{H}_1 + \Delta\hat{H}_2$$
- **Specific Enthalpy from $C_p(T)$ function (for air):**
    $$\Delta \hat{H}_{sens} = 33.46 + 0.688 \times 10^{-2} t + 0.7604 \times 10^{-5} t^2 - 3.593 \times 10^{-9} t^3$$
- **Mass Balance at Steady State:**
    $$\dot{m}_{in} = \dot{m}_{out}$$
