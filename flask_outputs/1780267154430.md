**Problem 1 – Liquid nitrogen is used in a number of medical applications, such as removing tumors during surgery. Suppose gaseous N2 at room temperature [highlighted in yellow: (298 K)] is cooled to liquid [red] > gas [black] N2 just below the boiling point [highlighted in yellow: (77 K)]. The [highlighted in cyan: heat of vaporization,] the specific enthalpy change from the [highlighted in yellow: liquid to the vapor form, of nitrogen [blue circle: is 1336 cal/mol]].**

What would be the overall change in specific enthalpy for this process?

  $$N_2 \text{ (g)} \text{ @ } 77\text{ K} \xrightarrow{\text{[dashed red arrow pointing to a diagonal cyan-highlighted bar]} \Delta\hat{H}_2} N_2 \text{ (l)} \text{ @ } 77\text{ K}$$

- Handwritten Calculations:
  - $$\Delta\hat{H}_1 = 298\text{ K to } 77\text{ K} = -1435\text{ cal/mol}$$
  - $$\Delta\hat{H}_2 = \text{Phase change (condensation)} = -1336\text{ cal/mol}$$
  - $$\Delta\hat{H} = \Delta\hat{H}_1 + \Delta\hat{H}_2$$
  - $$= -1435\text{ cal/mol} + (-1336\text{ cal/mol})$$
  - $$= \boxed{-2771\text{ cal/mol}}$$
  - $$\underline{\underline{\text{exothermic}}}$$

# 📌 Key Concepts
- **Enthalpy Change ($\Delta\hat{H}$):** The change in energy of a system at constant pressure. Specific enthalpy change ($\Delta\hat{H}$) refers to the change per unit mass or mole.
- **Hess's Law:** The total enthalpy change for a process is independent of the pathway taken. This allows for calculating overall enthalpy changes by summing the enthalpy changes of a series of hypothetical intermediate steps.
- **Heat of Vaporization ($\Delta\hat{H}_{vap}$):** The specific enthalpy change required to transform a substance from a liquid to a gas at its boiling point, at constant temperature and pressure. The reverse process, condensation, has an enthalpy change of $-\Delta\hat{H}_{vap}$.
- **Heat Capacity ($C_p$):** The amount of heat required to change the temperature of a substance by a unit degree at constant pressure. It can be a constant value for liquids/solids over moderate temperature ranges or a temperature-dependent function for gases, often expressed as a polynomial.
- **Integration of Heat Capacity:** The enthalpy change associated with a temperature change for a substance with a temperature-dependent heat capacity is calculated by integrating $C_p(T)$ over the temperature range: $\Delta\hat{H} = \int_{T_1}^{T_2} C_p(T) dT$.
- **Exothermic/Endothermic Processes:** Exothermic processes release energy (heat), resulting in a negative enthalpy change ($\Delta\hat{H} < 0$). Endothermic processes absorb energy, resulting in a positive enthalpy change ($\Delta\hat{H} > 0$).

# 📐 Key Equations
- **Overall Enthalpy Change (Hess's Law):**
  $$ \Delta\hat{H}_{\text{total}} = \sum \Delta\hat{H}_{\text{steps}} $$
- **Enthalpy Change due to Temperature Change (constant $C_p$):**
  $$ \Delta\hat{H} = C_p \Delta T $$
- **Enthalpy Change due to Temperature Change (temperature-dependent $C_p$):**
  $$ \Delta\hat{H} = \int_{T_1}^{T_2} (a + bT + cT^2 + dT^3) dT $$
- **Enthalpy Change for Phase Change (Condensation):**
  $$ \Delta\hat{H}_{\text{condensation}} = -\Delta\hat{H}_{\text{vaporization}} $$
