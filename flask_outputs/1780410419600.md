Problem Set 3.2

**CONSERVATION OF MASS IN STEADY-STATE NON-REACTING SYSTEMS WITH MULTIPLE INLETS AND OUTLETS**
**Problem 1**
Two cylindrical venous capillary vessels join to form a larger cylindrical venule to return blood to the heart. The first vessel has a diameter of 0.0006 cm, and an average blood flow velocity of 0.07 cm/s. The second vessel has a diameter of 0.0007 cm and an average blood flow velocity of 0.08 cm/s. The diameter of the collecting vessel is 0.0008 cm.
- Blood density: $\rho_{\text{blood}} = 1.056\text{ g/cm}^3$

Find the average blood flow velocity and the mass flow rate of blood in the collecting vessel. Assume blood density is unchanged.

- **Assumptions:**
    1.  Density = unchanged
    2.  Steady-state
    3.  Open-system
    4.  No reactions
    5.  STP
- **Draw:** A diagram of the system
- **Diagram Variables:**
    - $\dot{M}_1 \Rightarrow d_1 = 6 \times 10^{-4}\text{ cm}$, $v_1 = 0.07\text{ cm/s}$
    - $\dot{M}_2 \Rightarrow d_2 = 7 \times 10^{-4}\text{ cm}$, $v_2 = 0.08\text{ cm/s}$
    - $\dot{M}_3 = \quad d = 8 \times 10^{-4}\text{ cm}$
- **[IMAGE: Vessel Junction Schematic]:** A control volume with a dashed outline showing two inlet arrows ($\dot{M}_1$, $\dot{M}_2$) and one outlet arrow ($\dot{M}_3$). Circled step numbers ①, ②, and ③ point to the respective inlet and outlet ports.

Step ①:
- $$\boxed{\dot{M}_1 + \dot{M}_2 = \dot{M}_3}$$
- $$A_1 v_1 + A_2 v_2 = A_3 v_3$$
- $$\pi r_1^2 v_1 + \pi r_2^2 v_2 = \pi r_3^2 v_3$$
- $$\pi \left( \frac{6 \times 10^{-4}\text{ cm}}{2} \right)^2 (0.07\text{ cm/s}) + \pi \left( \frac{7 \times 10^{-4}\text{ cm}}{2} \right)^2 (0.08\text{ cm/s}) = \pi \left( \frac{8 \times 10^{-4}\text{ cm}}{2} \right)^2 v_3$$
- $$1.979 \times 10^{-8} \text{ cm}^3\text{/s} + 3.077 \times 10^{-8} \text{ cm}^3\text{/s} = 5.027 \times 10^{-7} \text{ cm}^2 v_3$$
- $$5.056 \times 10^{-8} \text{ cm}^3\text{/s} = 5.027 \times 10^{-7} \text{ cm}^2 (v_3)$$
- $$\boxed{v_3 = 0.101\text{ cm/s}}$$

Step ②:
- $$\dot{M}_1 + \dot{M}_2 = \dot{M}_3$$
- $$\rho_1 \dot{V}_1 + \rho_2 \dot{V}_2 = \rho_3 \dot{V}_3$$
- $$\rho \dot{V}_1 + \rho \dot{V}_2 = \rho \dot{V}_3$$
- $$1.056\text{ g/cm}^3 \left(1.979 \times 10^{-8}\text{ cm}^3\text{/s}\right) + 1.056\text{ g/cm}^3 \left(3.077 \times 10^{-8}\text{ cm}^3\text{/s}\right) = 1.056\text{ g/cm}^3 \left(5.056 \times 10^{-8}\text{ cm}^3\text{/s}\right)$$
- $$2.089 \times 10^{-8}\text{ g/s} + 3.249 \times 10^{-8}\text{ g/s} = 5.338 \times 10^{-8}\text{ g/s}$$
- $$\boxed{5.338 \times 10^{-8}\text{ g/s} = 5.338 \times 10^{-8}\text{ g/s}}$$

**Problem 2**
Let's reconsider the coronary artery problem for Tuesday's class. Turns out our assumption of no blood accumulation in the plaque region was wrong and the patient has developed coronary ischemia (blood flow is obstructed at that position). Patients with coronary ischemia may undergo a surgical treatment called coronary artery bypass grafting, which uses a blood-vessel graft to connect the segments of the vessel with normal blood flow to redirect blood around the blocked points. The patient's surgeon wants to implant a graft to bypass the constricted region (see figure) and reaches out to you as the cardiovascular engineer to design an appropriate synthetic graft.

Remember, the diameter of a healthy coronary artery is 2.5mm and the diameter of the area with a plaque decreased by two-thirds for this patient. The blood flow rate in a healthy artery is 6.4 cm/s and the density of blood is 1.056 g/cm3. The plaque decreased the blood flow velocity in that section of the artery by 25%. Assume no reactions with blood and the curvature of the graft does not alter blood flow meaning blood flows at the same velocity through the graft as in the healthy artery. Our goal is to return the system to steady state.

a. Draw a diagram of the system (with input and output streams).
b. Write the accounting equation for tracking the mass of blood.
c. What should be the mass flow rate through the graft?
d. What must be the diameter of the graft to achieve your goal?

- **[IMAGE: Coronary Artery Bypass Graft]:** A detailed anatomical illustration showing a major branching coronary artery on the surface of the heart with a curved bypass vessel labeled "Graft" attached. A dashed line enclosing the bifurcation and graft represents the "System boundary." Labels indicate "Healthy section D = 2.5 mm, v = 6.4 cm/s" for both incoming and outgoing healthy segments, and a point "B" at the upper bifurcation junction.
- Conversion: $\boxed{1\text{ mm} = 0.1\text{ cm}}$

**a.**
- **[IMAGE: Schematic Diagram for Problem 2]:** A dashed blue box represents the system boundary with three red arrows: $\dot{M}_1$ entering from the left, $\dot{M}_2$ entering from the top, and $\dot{M}_3$ exiting to the right.
- **Notes for streams:**
    - $\dot{m}_1 = \text{Pl}$ (Plaque section): Diameter $D_1 = (2.5\text{ mm}) \times (1 - 2/3) = 2.5\text{ mm} \times (1/3) = 0.83\text{ mm} \Rightarrow 0.083\text{ cm}$
    - Velocity $v_1 = 6.4\text{ cm/s} \times 0.75 = 4.8\text{ cm/s}$
    - $\dot{m}_2 = \text{g}$ (Graft section): Diameter $D_2 = \boxed{?\text{ cm}}$
    - Velocity $v_2 = 6.4\text{ cm/s}$ (same as healthy artery)

**b.**
- $$\boxed{\dot{M}_1 + \dot{M}_2 = \dot{M}_3}$$

**c.**
- $$\dot{m}_1 + \dot{m}_2 = \dot{m}_3$$
- $$\rho \dot{V}_1 + \rho \dot{V}_2 = \rho \dot{V}_3$$
- $$\pi \left( \frac{0.083\text{ cm}}{2} \right)^2 \cdot 4.8\text{ cm/s} + \pi \left( \frac{D_2}{2} \right)^2 \cdot 6.4\text{ cm/s} = \pi \left( \frac{0.25\text{ cm}}{2} \right)^2 \cdot 6.4\text{ cm/s}$$
- Multiplying by $\rho = 1.056\text{ g/cm}^3$ for each term:
    - Term 1: $\left( \pi \left( \frac{0.083}{2} \right)^2 \cdot 4.8\text{ cm/s} \right) \times 1.056\text{ g/cm}^3 = 0.0274\text{ g/s}$
    - Term 2: Represents the unknown mass flow rate $x$ through the graft.
    - Term 3: $\left( \pi \left( \frac{0.25\text{ cm}}{2} \right)^2 \cdot 6.4\text{ cm/s} \right) \times 1.056\text{ g/cm}^3 = 0.331\text{ g/s}$
- $$0.0274\text{ g/s} + x = 0.331\text{ g/s}$$
- $$\boxed{\dot{m}_2 = x = 0.304\text{ g/s}}$$

**d.**
- We know the mass flow rate through the graft: $\dot{m}_2 = 0.304\text{ g/s}$
- $$\dot{m}_2 = \rho v_2 A_2$$
- $$0.304\text{ g/s} = 1.056\frac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3} \cdot \pi \left( \frac{D_2}{2} \right)^2 \cdot 6.4\text{ cm/s}$$
- $$\frac{0.304\text{ g/s}}{1.056\text{ g/cm}^3} = \pi \left( \frac{D_2}{2} \right)^2 \cdot 6.4\text{ cm/s}$$
- $$0.288\text{ cm}^3\text{/s} = \pi \left( \frac{D_2}{2} \right)^2 \cdot 6.4\text{ cm/s}$$
- $$\frac{0.288\text{ cm}^3\text{/s}}{6.4\text{ cm/s}} = \pi \left( \frac{D_2}{2} \right)^2$$
- $$0.0449\text{ cm}^2 = \pi \left( \frac{D_2}{2} \right)^2$$
- $$\frac{0.0449\text{ cm}^2}{\pi} = \left( \frac{D_2}{2} \right)^2$$
- $$0.0143\text{ cm}^2 = \left( \frac{D_2}{2} \right)^2$$
- $$\sqrt{0.0143\text{ cm}^2} = \frac{D_2}{2}$$
- $$0.1197\text{ cm} = \frac{D_2}{2}$$
- $$\boxed{D_2 = 0.239\text{ cm}}$$

# 📐 Global Key Equations

    $$\sum \dot{M}_{in} = \sum \dot{M}_{out}$$
- Mass Flow Rate:
    $$\dot{M} = \rho \dot{V}$$
- Volumetric Flow Rate:
    $$\dot{V} = Av$$
- Area of a circle (for vessel cross-section):
    $$A = \pi r^2 = \pi \left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^2$$
