1.2 Electric Fields
Electric Field Definition
Electric field $\vec{E}$ at a point P is defined as the electrostatic force $\vec{F}$ experienced by a small positive test charge $q_0$ placed at P, divided by the charge itself.
[blue]The force of the field acting on a point charge in the field
$$\vec{E} = \frac{\vec{F}}{q_0}$$
The direction of $\vec{E}$ is the same as the direction of $\vec{F}$ because $q_0$ is a positive scalar.
[blue]Direction of E (field vector) is the same as the direction of force on a positive charge.
[blue]Units of E are N/C (Newtons/Coulomb)

Electric Field of a Point Charge
Consider a point charge $q$ located at the origin.
[blue]Source charge
The magnitude of the electric field $\vec{E}$ at a distance $r$ from $q$ is:
$$E = k \frac{|q|}{r^2}$$
[IMAGE: Diagram showing a positive charge +q at the origin, with electric field lines radiating outwards. A point P is shown at distance r from +q, and an electric field vector E is drawn pointing away from +q at P. A test charge +q0 is also shown near P, with a force F vector pointing away from +q.]
[blue]If source charge $q$ is (+), then $\vec{E}$ points away from $q$.
[blue]If source charge $q$ is (-), then $\vec{E}$ points towards $q$.
[blue]E is a vector, so need direction.
[blue]Field is always directed away from + charge (positive source)
[blue]Field is always directed towards - charge (negative source)

The direction of $\vec{E}$ is radially outward if $q$ is positive, and radially inward if $q$ is negative.
In vector form:
$$\vec{E} = k \frac{q}{r^2} \hat{r}$$
[blue]$\hat{r}$ is a unit vector pointing from source charge $q$ to the point where E is being calculated.
Note that if $q$ is negative, then $\vec{E}$ is in the direction opposite to $\hat{r}$, which means $\vec{E}$ points towards $q$.
[blue]This equation automatically gives the correct direction.
