Definition of learning
A computer program is said to learn from experience E with respect to some task T and some performance measure P, if its performance on T, as measured by P, improves with experience E.
Example: email spam filter
    - Task T: classify emails as spam or not spam
    - Experience E: watching you label emails as spam/not spam
    - Performance P: fraction of emails correctly classified

2.1 – The Schrödinger Equation (Time-Independent)

*   Let's consider a free particle moving in 1-D in a region where $V(x) = 0$.
    [blue] $V=0$ (no forces acting on it) [dark gray] (a free particle has no forces acting on it).
*   In classical mechanics, the total energy of such a particle is given by $E = \frac{p^2}{2m}$.
    [blue] $E=K.E.$
*   In quantum mechanics, we replace momentum and energy with operators.
    [blue] $p \rightarrow \hat{p}$, $E \rightarrow \hat{E}$.

[blue] $\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Psi = \psi \frac{d\phi}{dt}$
[blue] $\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}\Psi = \phi \frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2}$
Separation of variables: Let's assume that the wave function $\Psi(x,t)$ can be separated into a product of a function of position only $\psi(x)$ and a function of time only $\phi(t)$.
$\Psi(x,t) = \psi(x)\phi(t)$
[blue] product of two independent functions.
Let's substitute this back into the Schrödinger equation:
$\hat{p} = -i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}$
$\hat{E} = i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}$
So, the Schrödinger equation for a free particle in 1-D is:
$i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Psi(x,t) = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2}\Psi(x,t)$
[blue] This is the time-dependent Schrödinger equation for a free particle.

$i\hbar\psi(x)\frac{d\phi}{dt} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\phi(t)\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2}$
Divide by $\Psi(x,t) = \psi(x)\phi(t)$:
$\frac{i\hbar}{\phi}\frac{d\phi}{dt} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{1}{\psi}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2}$
[blue] LHS = function of $t$ only, RHS = function of $x$ only.
[blue] Therefore both sides must equal a constant. (which we call E)
This expression must hold for all $x$ and $t$. This is only possible if both sides are equal to the same constant. We will call this constant $E$ (for energy).
[dark gray] Let's call it 'E' because it relates to energy.

Time-dependent part:
$\frac{i\hbar}{\phi}\frac{d\phi}{dt} = E$
[blue] Time-dependent part
Time-independent part:
$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{1}{\psi}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} = E$
[blue] Time-independent part

Time-dependent part:
$i\hbar\frac{d\phi}{dt} = E\phi$
We can solve this differential equation by separating variables:
$\int\frac{d\phi}{\phi} = \int-\frac{iE}{\hbar}dt$
This gives
$\ln\phi = -\frac{iE}{\hbar}t + C$
Or
$\phi(t) = Ae^{-\frac{iE}{\hbar}t}$
[blue] $A=e^C$
where $A$ is an integration constant.

Time-independent part:
$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} = E\psi$
[blue] This is the Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation for a free particle.
[blue] Often written as $\hat{H}\psi = E\psi$.
[blue] $\hat{H}$ is the Hamiltonian operator.
